## Notes
- An image is defined as a graphical representation of the spacial distribution of one of its properties
- Typically to characterize an object, you would need a radiation field that has a wavelength similar or smaller than the smallest feature of the object (for resolution purpose I think?)
- A technique called induced local interactions can be used instead to combat this wavelength limitation
    * A second field restrictions the interaction of the object with the first field to a limited region
    * This makes the resolution independent of the wavelength, now it's a function of the ration between the normal width of interaction to the shift produced by a gradiant in the second field
    * Called this technique zeugmatography
- From what I understand, you would scan with low power, and then high power and take the difference in scans. I believe power refers to the second field application.
    * From there, you have an image which will have an independence from wavelength
    * This was all derived from Figure 3, might be wrong
- Zeugmatographic techniques should have useful applications in studies relating to internal structures, states, and compositions of microscopic objects
